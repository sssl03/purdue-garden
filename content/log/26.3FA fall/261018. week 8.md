### teaching work

monday and tuesday were holidays, so this was a short week.
#### [[exlab (26.3FA)]]

*lecture.* using [my work with tata studi](https://setwrite.in/projects/202207-study-app.html) as an example, i explained how, ==with each iteration, designers raise fidelity or resolution or both==. **resolving work**, to me, involves things like handling edge-cases. fidelity is about being faithful, and so **raising fidelity** involves making something appear-and-behave more like how it will finally be experienced.[^1]

i think this was the first time i made 'slides' to explain a concept to students. it helped me stay on track, and keep the lecturing short ; so, i'm glad i did. 

![[Pasted image 20261021181138.png]]

this was followed by *much conversation.* i worked with students to help them objectively bridge *problem framing* to *ideation/prototyping*. some students jump too far ahead, others suffer *stuckness* (usually caused by a lack of *focus* in their framing), and yet others are unable to articulate their intuition unto others ; so, i work with each one to *tune* their progress.

some students visited the [[purdue nursing simlab]] to watch nursing students undergo a simulation-training with [[laerdal]] manikins.

#### [[behaviour hacking project (26–27)]]

we discussed problems (from our own lived experiences) which we will try and *hack* in the weeks ahead. [[armita]] shared how she controls her punctuality through dozens of pre-set alarms, and [[mabel]] discussed the procrastination we all experience in arranging clothes or washing utensils. 

the root of problem isn't always obvious in its symptoms, so i asked the students to think deeper and sideways to identify *insights* (as they *sensemake* through their problems).

i also emphasised how just making "stuff" is not enough ; as designers, we should \[aim to\] fix people's **behaviours** and not simply the **things** they use.

lastly: i encouraged them to ignore "process", and pick whatever they want to do as their next step for the project. if they feel like prototyping a solution, they can ; if they feel like studying the problem in greater detail, they can do that too. as long as they don't get "stuck" — *just* thinking, or *just* making, or *just* doing research — and keep shuffling between each such activity, they will be fine. i showed them this sketch ; although i know there are issues with many of the terms in there (and the representation is incomplete too), it's still better to show this to students than to just ramble words to them.

[^3]: [[jignesh]] wrote (in his [[pedagogical pocketbook]]) how 'education' comes from the latin *educere* which means 'drawing out from within'. i too have written about how education should be about self-discovery. learning who you are (or even what you can do) necessarily involves *play* — testing limits, trying to overcome boundaries, feeling a thrill or unease, etc —, and we intuitively say "this place lacks culture" when we sense it's absence.
