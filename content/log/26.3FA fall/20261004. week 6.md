#### [[exlab (26.3FA)]]

by the end of last week, students had identified several ~~problems~~ opportunities in [[laerdal]]'s lleap software. so i'd asked them to present their insights this week. 

> **findings** describe human behaviour, whereas **insights** describe the reasons behind that behaviour.[^3]

![[260929-233630-_9290163.jpg]]

![[260929-235655-_9290182.jpg]]

however, they struggled to present their observations in the form of insights, and very quickly dovetailed into describing ideas and solutions instead. (several of their observations and ideas excited me, though; so i did get quite happy even if i wasn't content.)

i realised three things: 
1. it is unfair for me to expect them to put in substantial work outside class.
2. students don't respond well to open-ended assignments.[^2] 
3. even though they've gone through several courses (many of which involved industry-sponsored projects), they really haven't learned how to move *objectively* from the discovery to ideation phase in a project.

(i may want and expect them to, and they likely should, but it is clear that most students in this programme do not; so i need to get my head out of my ass and respond to the reality i am confronted with. i know the students are capable; so—for now at-least!—i just need to find other ways to help them express and hone their abilities.)

so, i shifted gears, decided to work with them step-by-step, and helped them move from "observations" to "ideas". (i've done this quite intuitively all my life without ever realising that there was a method to it ; so ==teaching is forcing me to reflect more deeply on *how* i do *what* i do well, so that i may help others do it too==.) 
- i studied all the stuff they presented last week, identified common patterns in it, and shared it with them. 
  ![[Pasted image 20261004221339.png]]
- made them do a [5-why's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_whys) exercise in class to go deeper below the observations.
- shared concepts and theories for them to read about: eg. [modes](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modes/), and the tirade against modes in the latter half of [[inventing on principle (talk, bret victor, 2012)]]; [fitt's law](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/fitts-law/); and [other concepts described on bruce tognazzini's blog](https://asktog.com/atc/principles-of-interaction-design/).
- suggested using [heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) to arrive at keywords around which they can—next week—pivot from observations to ideas. 
- introduced them to [jira stories](https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/user-stories) (and how they are not different from [how might we](https://ixdf.org/literature/topics/how-might-we) statements), which can be used to frame a focus (i.e., define a goal before diving into ideation).

i also explained how they can use storyboards or journey maps to communicate their understanding to others, but shouldn't get hung up on terminology or templates.

#### [[uxfun (26.3FA)]]
i started the week with a lecture on this: while design is sold as a process, ==designers should not reduce the 'approach' to a 'process'==. just because a project-manager labels the first phase of a project as 'research' doesn't mean that *research* only happens there. designers have to do whatever is necessary—and whenever needed—to move away from uncertainty, curiosity, doubt, bias, lack of resolution, ambiguity, etc (while respecting a project's constraints).

then we **reframed** our project-briefs to be focused and goal-oriented. that allowed us to segue into a ~~*crazy-eights*~~ *rapid-eights*[^6] ideation exercise where each member in a group drew eight ideas in eight minutes (ish). then they discussed and selected one idea to move forward with.

#### [[behaviour hacking (26–27)]]

we heard [a 2018 episode on rituals from bbc radio 4's 'the digital human' podcast](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b0978nf1). our discussions post comprised words like alignment, structure, memorableness, transition. (made a note to start any future behaviour-hacking project/course with this podcast.)

![[IMG_1695.jpeg]]

once we start to ==look at rituals as “vehicles of affect”== (because they provide structure for people to reliably and memorably transition from one emotional state to another[^1]), we can treat rituals as fungible, mutable, *design*'able artefacts. 

as designers, we are goal-oriented: our machinations help transport the person’s emotions to a desired (goal) state while being context-aware, meaning-sensitive, and constraint-abiding. 

rituals, themselves, are simply a subset of human behaviours. they are a convenient means to introduce students to behaviour-hacking since it is easy to identify all aspects of a *behaviour-system* in them. so, deconstructing rituals is a good starting point for behaviour hacking.

a *behaviour system* is a term i just made up to refer to a the designer's understanding of...
- a situation: a person, a context and scenario, a goal.
- a choreography: what and how a person senses, interprets, understands, decides, expresses, and acts.
- and why any of this is ==meaningful== to a person.
