### teaching work

#### [[exlab (26.3FA)]]

continued working with students as they move from *problem framing* to *ideation/prototyping*. some students are beginning to enjoy it (especially the ones working on tangible prototypes), but i also notice how some students aren't producing sufficient output. 

this leaves me with very little to build on (to give them feedback, and to push them one step ahead). this may be because i've not defined clear rubrics or sharp deadlines ; but the cause is likely more distributed (and also elsewhere). i can not do much else right now : in the future, though, i may apply my learnings from [[impact course]] (and [[self-determination theory]]) on this course too.

![[Screenshot 2026-10-26 at 6.54.09 pm.png]]

i keep pushing students to focus on a small aspect of a problem, and instead of making some grand fix, do something small but do it well. 

i recently spotted this: [["do small things with great love"]].

#### [[uxfun (26.3FA)]]

students began a 2½-week project based on terms—affordance, signifiers, feedback, etc—introduced last week.

![[Pasted image 20261026201637.png]]

joked about how—after learning about terms like signifiers, feedback, affordance, etc—i've grown so adept at noticing and articulating problems in every little thing around me, that anyone i date turns into a mental wreck because i keep highlighting flaws in them[^1].

![[Pasted image 20261027011630.png]]

[[sienna wheeler]] collected midterm feedback from [[uxfun (26.3FA)]]'s students: some felt like they're not learning much (and that the course it just feels like common sense), and some were happy because they're learning to apply concepts. on the face of it, the feedback seemed bipolar, but i think i understand what this means. i am still confident that my approach (of keeping things jargon-free, and helping students hone *an approach* through conversations and small projects) is better than just inundating them with concepts, theories, readings. however, even if students learn things, they need to get the impression of learning (and: feel more competent, and relate the content with their own interests) — and these are things i can do better. i'm working on these while redesigning [[(course) ux fundamentals]] in the [[impact course]].

#### [[impact course]]

i'm working on a revised course-plan for [[(course) ux fundamentals]] through this course. i've completed planning a section of the course (based largely on my experience teaching [[uxfun (26.3FA)]] this semester), and enjoyed discussing it with [[annette benson]]. i am quite eager to build on it further. 

i am slowly warming up to the idea of assessments (in the form of single-line rubrics).

![[Pasted image 20261026175330.png]]

spoke with [[chantal levesque-bristol]] about the history—and possible future—of [foundational learning outcomes (flo's)](https://www.purdue.edu/senate/committees/standing-committees/educational/curr/outcomes.php) at [[purdue]]. when i was new to purdue, i received a lot of grief from someone when i tried to change the content in [[(course) ux fundamentals]]. they kept telling me that the course should be taught in a certain way because it needs to comply with flo's (etc); but they never explained what those are, why they came about to be, or how much room i am afforded to experiment with them. so the conversation with chantal really helped, because now i won't feel as helpless if someone tries to use that terminology to control what and how i teach. [[rua mae williams]] also keeps reminding me that i have academic freedom ; but since free-but-ignorant people are easy to manipulate — as i was —, i am working hard to fill gaps in knowledge that i once felt exploited on.

### social

last week, when teaching students the concept of *affordance*, i commented how we no longer *afford* each other the chance to start a conversation with strangers. i talked about [missed connections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missed_connection); and lamented about the growth of loneliness in society[^2]. i described how—just that morning—i'd tried informing a passer-by that their bag was open, but since they had airpods on they continued walking like a zombie ; and i felt upset about how airpods've made it harder for people to approach each other, seek assistance, or make small gestures (of kindness, or otherwise). i've tried all semester to wear airpods to experience the world like so many others around me do ; and i hate them. the one time i didn't have them on, a random girl who was walking behind me said she liked my hat ; i smiled and told her my partner ([[miss]]!) made it for me ; and i wondered how many such opportunities to smile i may have been deaf to this semester. in closing, i commented how people—with their airpods on in public—no longer *afford* each other chance conversations. "no wonder everyone's lonely!"
![[Pasted image 20261026230019.png]]

at the end of the week, i passed [[armita]] by on the street. she was looking elsewhere as she walked past. both of us were wearing airpods, though, so i did not bother calling-out to her. if she'd noticed me and called out from behind, i wouldn't know. "airpods kill-off two more smiles", a headline flashed in my head. some americans are quite obsessed with saving *unborn childs* ; i wonder if airpods killing *unborn smiles* is close enough a problem for them to also focus on?[^1]
