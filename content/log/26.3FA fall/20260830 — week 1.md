#### work: teaching

[[exlab (26.3FA)]]:
- finalised project briefs. prepared a course-syllabus and schedule. i have nine students (most of who are from [[purdue ux design]]), which is an excellent number to have for a pilot instance of the course. 
- the students shared the things they wish they had done more of, and that reaffirmed the need for this course. so i felt happy, because i knew the students would be motivated to make work. 
  ![[Pasted image 20260907205532.png]]
- since most of their studies/work focused on a 'design researcher' skillset (interviews, questionnaires, etc leading up to lo-fi prototypes), i shared extracts from [erika hall's book, just enough research](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=erika+hall+just+enough+research&ia=web) to help them see how making is also a form of research. i also shared examples from my own work, to explain how making can communicate ideas powerfully.
- i am laying groundwork to bring them to a point where they will see design not as a "process to follow" but as something that *emerges* when a person oscillates (iteratively, fluidly, reason'ably) between three things:
	- **uncertainty**: curiosity, assumptions, doubt, skepticism
	- **prototypes**: sketching, writing
	- **evidence**: specificity, facts, tests, evaluation
- we watched [every frame a painting's video, "how does an editor think and feel"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Q3eITC01Fg), and discussed this: an editor will tell you that their job is about the eyes, and about timing; but they can not tell you the exact formula for how to work with these, and simply learning the tools does not guarantee a good edit; the only way to get better at it is to practice (loads and loads!).
