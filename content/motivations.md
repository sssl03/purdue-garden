i am writing this note in fall 2026.

last semester, i encouraged my graduate students (in [[purdue ux design]]) to *make-in-public* their capstone projects, and invited [[arjun-itp]] introduce them to writing.

![[export-260116-6.jpg]]

![[export-260116-5.jpg]]

he also ran a short tutorial on 'digital gardening', and helped some students get started with obsidian–quartz. students mostly used notion or wix (etc), and i don't recall any of them publicising their logs; but, them writing _something_ regularly helped me guide them better. only at the end of the course did some of them recognise the value this brought to them, with one comment being:

> i'm happy to share ... that posting/making work in public in a good way works.

while reflecting on his own garden, arjun wrote so: "i realized the importance of writing (and sharing) in public. i would have random conversations with people, just because they read a thought that i posted on this blog; or see someone figure out an electronic-component by going through my experiments-log." 

i remain hopeful that my students at [[purdue]] get infected by writing, or in other ways see value in making-in-public. in part, this is why i am getting back to writing a public log after a long break away: so that i may practice what i preach.
