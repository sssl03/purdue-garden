last semester, i encouraged graduate students to #make-in-public during their capstone projects. i invited [[arjun-itp]] to introduce the class to obsidian–quartz, but students mostly used notion or wix (etc) for their logs. i don't recall any student publicising their log, but them writing _something_—even when done poorly—helped me guide them better. 

while reflecting on his own *garden*, [[arjun-itp]] wrote so: "i realized the importance of writing (and sharing) in public. i would have random conversations with people, just because they read a thought that i posted on this blog; or see someone figure out an electronic-component by going through my experiments-log." 

i raise an eyebrow at how he's spelt 'realized', but his sentiment is undeniable. i wish his observations infect some of my students here at [[purdue university]]. in part, this is why i am getting back to writing a public log after a long break away. the other part why is that writing keeps me reflective, motivated, and accountable; and will perhaps contribute to me having less awful a year this time around than the last one was.
