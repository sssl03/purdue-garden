notwithstanding the fact that people like me were *invited* to come work in the united states by american companies/institutes *because* of our expertise, the vice-president of the united states said this:

> you cannot lay off american workers and replace them with foreign ==indentured servants==[^1]

i am most-certainly *not* an indentured servant, because i can walk away from my 'master' at any point if i so wish; and i also can not be sold by one american employer to another. but that statement is still dangerous and hurtful, because now that such beliefs are widespread—even though not true—, they are hurting people[^5] in very tangible ways. so, my initial reaction to his statements was a feeling of anger (stemming from fear).

but then i sat down to reflect on this.

while i might be enticed to dismiss that statement as being one sick man's comment (and not a reflection of the country's stance), i also know that he is able to say and do such things because he draws his power from both the actions and inactions of the people, institutions, and ideologies in this country right now.

i read about the differences between 'slaves' and 'indentured servants' — and while there are key differences, both these terms hark back to the time when slavery was common in america. that made me muse about how the american vice-president, through that comment, was using ideas that american ==enslavers== were associated with in the past. 

that realisation transformed my fear and anger into a feeling of pity. because, what else can one feel for someone who paints such a dim view of themselves?

i subsequently read about the united states's recent decision to livestream an execution[^2]; and also about the country sanctioning the international court of justice[^3][^7]. the united states is a very powerful country, but seems to be—unfortunately—very confused right now. "why do peoples who have *so much* still act *so small*?", i wondered aloud to [[robert shaw]].

even though i am a rather meaningless indentured servant from a hellhole country[^6], i can't help but feel some ==pity== for it[^4].
