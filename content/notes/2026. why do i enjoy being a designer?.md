an exchange with [[ann kim]] in [[20260927. week 5]] triggered me to briefly wonder whether or not i like design. 

the thing is: i genuinely dislike designers and design educators (because they are largely lazy, insecure, selfish, and uncaring[^3] for the people and scenarios they design/teach for). and so i often speak quite critically about the field of design. however, i know that there is a distinction between what older designers *have done* and what younger designers *can do* if they are trained well ; and unfortunately i remain deeply infatuated to design's potential (even if most of it has been deftly squandered).

i was reminded of a paragraph i'd once written[^1] ; and, since i was sat alone at my desk then, i allowed myself to tear-up a bit after i read it:

> machines can help us move quicker than the fastest person alive, be more precise than the most fastidious watchmaker, and make sounds that no finger could strum. and yet, we like running, crafting things and strumming guitars.

you see: i don't want to make the best guitar, watch, robot. i want to focus on the emotions a person feels when they experience sensations, time, movement. as a designer, i can function simultaneously as an artist and engineer, without ever needing to separate my work and personal life[^4].

this is why i like operating as a designer: because i get to work with how people feel[^2], and have no need to separate my work from my (human-)condition. 

[^1]: from my writeup describing the (impossible) [collaboration for expression](https://setwrite.in/projects/201511-rca-collaboration-for-expression.html) project (2016)

[^2]: [what do designers work with?](https://setwrite.in/reflections/202307-what-do-designers-work-with.html) (2023)

[^3]: i am sure people in other fields are so too (because, *duh*), but since i identify as a designer i feel obligated to mete out my sharpest criticism at my own 'kind'.

[^4]: the idea of 'work-life balance' and the person who came up with it, both, are two pieces of the same 💩.
