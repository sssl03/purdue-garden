when design-strategists work with companies to help distil vision-mission-goals (before diving into actual strategy or branding work), their techniques may be seen as a form of *friction*[^5] that forces the company to slow down and reflect on what its values are.[^6]

they can use ~~visual[^3]~~ *experiential*[^4] methods to gift *friction* to companies that wish to adapt to a world where the cheapening of making[^1] has made it more difficult to *be relatable*[^2] and *remain distinguishable*.

[^1]: eg. when people ~~make~~ *prompt and generate* logos, taglines, presentations, videos, illustrations, apps, websites, etc.

[^2]:  to be relatable both *internally* and *externally*: i.e., to employees (to help motivate them to produce good work, to stymie attrition, to attract talent, etc), and also to their consumers/clients.

[^3]: like: drawing while in meetings, and making illustrations to explain complex ideas.

[^4]: besides writing and drawing, their methods may involve sound (or silence), taste, movement, etc.

[^5]: while friction is often seen as a negative thing, it is important—and humbling—to appreciate how it would be impossible to walk, climb or make love to someone in its absence.

[^6]: *self-discovery* and *haste* do not a good cocktail make.
